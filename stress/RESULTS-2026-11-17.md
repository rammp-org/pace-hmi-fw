# RTPS stress results, 2026-11-17

## Test 1: Python stand-ins, today's limits vs the MIB's

**How it was tested**
- **The two ends:** the real HMI (a Tab5) is on the laptop's WiFi hotspot. Everything else
  runs on the laptop as "stand-ins": small Python programs (`scripts/rtps_stress.py`), each
  one an RTPS participant. A stand-in joins and discovers like a real device, but it isn't
  real ROS 2.
- **Who stands in for what** (from `stress/network.toml`):
  - **MIB stand-in:** always running.
  - **SPACE RACER boards:** 4 stand-ins.
  - **Jetson ROS nodes:** stand-ins named after Guo's nodes, with a ROS node's usual set of
    topics and services.
  - **Filler nodes:** more of the same, for higher counts.
- **What data goes where:**
  - **Stress data in:** every stand-in sends a 256-byte message on a test topic,
    `rt/rammp/stress/to_hmi`, to the HMI at a set rate. The HMI counts what arrives from each
    one, including gaps in the sequence numbers.
  - **Joystick out:** the HMI sends its joystick message as usual. Every stand-in that
    subscribes to it counts what arrives.
  - **MIB link:** the MIB stand-in sends MibStatus to the HMI twice a second and exchanges
    e-stop heartbeats with it, as the real MIB does.
- **How it's read:** once a second the HMI prints its counters (stress received per sender,
  MibStatus, matched peers, free memory) on USB serial. The script reads them there, so
  measuring adds no network traffic.
- **The sweep:**
  - For each participant count (2, 4 ... 24), the script steps the stress rate up: 1, 5, 10,
    20, 50, 100, 200 Hz per stand-in, 30 s per step, until a step fails.
  - A step passes when every stand-in's stream arrives at 99% or better, every subscriber
    gets at least 95% of the joystick messages the MIB gets, MibStatus loss is 5% or less, and
    the HMI didn't reset.
- **The two firmware builds compared:**
  - **Before:** today's limits (`stress/sdkconfig.stress`).
  - **Tuned:** the MIB's discovery limits on top (`stress/sdkconfig.tuned`).

| Participants | Matched before | Matched tuned | Max kB/s per participant, before | Max kB/s per participant, tuned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 15.8 (50 Hz) | 31.6 (100 Hz) |
| 4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 6.3 (20 Hz) | 15.8 (50 Hz) |
| 6 | 5/6 | 6/6 | 3.2 (10 Hz) | 6.3 (20 Hz) |
| 8 | 5/8 | 8/8 | 0.3 (1 Hz) | 3.2 (10 Hz) |
| 10 | 5/10 | 10/10 | under 0.3 | 6.3 (20 Hz) |
| 12 | 5/12 | 12/12 | 0.3 (1 Hz) | 3.2 (10 Hz) |
| 16 | 5/16 | 16/16 | 0.3 (1 Hz) | 1.6 (5 Hz) |
| 20 | 5/20 | 20/20 | 0.3 (1 Hz) | under 0.3 |
| 24 | 5/24 | 23/24 | under 0.3 | under 0.3 |

"Participants" doesn't count the MIB stand-in, which is always present. "Max" is the highest
rate at which every matched stream was received at 99% or better, the joystick reached every
subscriber, and MibStatus loss stayed at 5% or less.

### Findings

1. **The HMI matches at most 6 remote participants on today's limits.**
   - Those 6 are the MIB plus the first 5 to join. Everyone after that never exchanges data
     with the HMI in either direction, and the HMI logs nothing about it.
   - So the joystick reached only the MIB and the orchestrator.
   - The cause is espp's default of 6 proxies per endpoint, the same limit the MIB had to
     raise. With the MIB's limits the HMI matched every peer up to 24.
2. **The e-stop deadman trips under load.**
   - The MIB stand-in latched the e-stop with "hmi silent (deadman)" 9 times in the corrected
     "before" run, 21 times in the first run, and once in the tuned run.
   - The HMI's sends fail with "No buffer space available" once the WiFi co-processor's
     transmit queue (`ESP_HOSTED_SDIO_TX_Q_SIZE` = 20) is full, and `rt/estop` waits in the
     same queue as everything else. It fails safe, but on a chair it would stop.
3. **The total bandwidth ceiling is low, about 25–63 kB/s (80–200 packets/s) received by the
   HMI.** It shrinks as participants are added. This was measured over WiFi; rerun it on
   Ethernet.
4. **Running out of pools costs CPU.** On today's limits, the capacity-6 proxy pools fail
   about 100 times a second once more than 6 participants are up, and each failure prints a
   warning over the 115200-baud console.
5. **The tuning costs RAM.** The lowest free internal heap was 108 KB on today's limits and
   70 KB tuned, with 16 or more participants.
6. **Other limits seen:**
   - ROS 2 parameter-service names overflow the 64-byte topic name limit (fixed by tuning).
   - The tables of unmatched remote endpoints (50 each) fill at about 4 ROS 2 nodes, in both
     builds.
7. **Round trip p99 is mostly 70–230 ms in both builds.** It was 78–93 ms with only the MIB,
   so on this link it is mostly WiFi.

## Test 2: real ROS 2 (tuned firmware)

**How it was tested**
- **The setup:** the same as Test 1, but the ROS side is real ROS 2 Jazzy on Cyclone DDS,
  configured as on the robot: one named interface, multicast for discovery only
  (`stress/ros2/`, `rtps_stress.py rossweep`).
- **Always running on the laptop:**
  - **The MIB stand-in and the 4 SPACE RACER stand-ins:** still the Python programs, since
    the real boards run espp, not ROS.
  - **Guo's orchestrator and mocks** (rammp-orchestrator#1): 10 real ROS 2 processes running
    their demo. Their traffic to each other stays on the laptop. All the HMI gets from them is
    their discovery messages, which is the load we wanted from a real Jetson stack.
- **What's added step by step:** real rclpy nodes (`stress/ros2/ros_standin.py`). Each one
  sends the same stress message to the HMI and subscribes to the joystick, as in Test 1.
- **Measured and swept as in Test 1.**
- **"Participants" below** counts the racers plus the rclpy nodes. The total number of remote
  participants is that plus 11 (the MIB and Guo's 10).

| Participants | Total remote | Matched | Max kB/s per participant | RTT p99 at that rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 15 | 4/4 | 15.8 (50 Hz) | 78 ms |
| 6 | 17 | 6/6 | 3.2 (10 Hz) | 191 ms |
| 8 | 19 | 8/8 | 6.3 (20 Hz) | 238 ms |
| 10 | 21 | 10/10 | 6.3 (20 Hz) | 152 ms |
| 12 | 23 | 12/12 | 3.2 (10 Hz) | 369 ms |
| 14 | 25 | 14/14 | 3.2 (10 Hz) | 176 ms |
| 16 | 27 | 16/16 | 0.3 (1 Hz) | 186 ms |

- **Matching holds past 24 participants.** espp forgets participants it shares no topics with
  ("Remote-participant pool full: forgot an unused participant"), and Guo's mocks share none
  with the HMI.
- **The bandwidth ceiling matches the Python run:** 25–63 kB/s in total.
- **Past the ceiling, the MIB link collapses rather than degrading.** MibStatus loss reached
  34–54% in the first failing step.
- **The e-stop deadman latched 5 times.**
- **Round trip p99 is above 100 ms at most counts** with the Jetson stack up.
- **Starting Guo's 10 processes at once didn't disturb a freshly booted HMI.**

## Test 3: a rebooted espp board is ignored until its sequence numbers catch up

**How it was tested**
- **Found by accident:** a sweep was started right after another one ended.
- **What the restart looked like:** the MIB stand-in stopped and started again a few seconds
  later with the same identity (GUID), and its MibStatus sequence numbers began again at 1.
  That's what an espp board does when it reboots.
- **The receiver:** the real HMI, running our espp firmware.
- **How it was watched:** the HMI's MibStatus counter and "last MibStatus" age on USB serial.
- **Repeat test:** I repeated it on purpose, once after a fresh HMI reboot (no problem) and
  once restarting straight after a run (the problem came back).

**What the code does**

espp gives every participant a GUID built from the board's MAC address, participant ID and
domain (`Domain::generateGuidPrefix`), so a board keeps the same GUID across reboots.

A best-effort reader drops any sample numbered below what it expects next from a writer it
already knows (`StatelessReader`: `if (cacheChange.sn < it->expectedSN) return;`).

**The risk:** a board that reboots faster than the lease it announces comes back with the same
GUID and its sequence numbers back at 1.
- The default lease is 5 s. A watchdog reset on Ethernet with a static IP could plausibly be
  that fast.
- Its peers then drop everything it sends until the count passes the old one.
- For a MIB that had been up for an hour (MibStatus at 2 Hz), the HMI would ignore it for
  about another hour, while its SPDP announcements keep the stale entry alive.

**What the bench showed**
- The HMI reported the MIB writer as matched (`m_mib=1`), but took no MibStatus for over
  90 s, until the count caught up.
- Not yet tried with two real espp boards. The stand-in also announced a 20 s lease where
  espp announces 5 s, so a real board has to be back within about 5 s for this to happen.
- ROS 2 nodes aren't affected: Cyclone and Fast DDS use random GUIDs.

**Possible fixes in espp:**
- Mix boot-time entropy into the GUID prefix.
- Or reset a writer proxy's expected sequence number when its participant re-announces after a
  gap.
